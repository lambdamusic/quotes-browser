Ontologically subjective yet epistemically objective

Searle states, “Observer relativity implies ontological subjectivity but ontological subjectivity does not preclude epistemic objectivity.” To translate this into English: the existence of things that exist only because we believe them to exist does not mean that these things cannot be objective. Money is a perfect example. It only exists because we believe it does, but nevertheless it has an objective truth. Mathematics, like language, is observer-relative: its mode of existence is ontologically subjective in that it depends on conscious agents for its existence. But mathematics has epistemically objective truths.
